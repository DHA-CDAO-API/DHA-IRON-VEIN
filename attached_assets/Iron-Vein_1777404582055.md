# Blood Support Mission Assurance

**Predictive Resupply for USINDOPACOM Distributed Marine Operations**

*Prepared for Marine Corps logistics and sustainment hackathon discussion*

-----

## Executive Scenario

In a USINDOPACOM distributed maritime operations scenario, Marine forces are operating across multiple dispersed island and afloat nodes. A regional medical logistics hub supports several spokes, including forward Marine units, Role 2 medical elements, expeditionary medical sites, and afloat receiving platforms. The force is conducting contested littoral operations while facing long distances, limited lift, degraded communications, unpredictable casualty demand, and increasing pressure on medical supply routes.

The most fragile part of the network is not simply the number of blood products on hand. The risk is whether viable blood, blood components, and the supplies required to collect, test, store, transport, and transfuse blood can reach the right node before the commander loses freedom of maneuver. Blood support is perishable, temperature-sensitive, route-dependent, and operationally decisive. If a hub becomes constrained, the failure can cascade across the spoke network and rapidly reduce survivability at the point of injury.

The prototype, Blood Support Mission Assurance, is a decision-support tool that predicts when and where blood support will fail first across a distributed Marine logistics network. It uses DHA and operational logistics data to visualize blood inventory, expiration risk, cold-chain status, transportation reliability, casualty demand, and approved contingency sourcing options.

> **Commander’s Question:** Which distributed Marine node will lose viable blood support first, why, and what action buys back the most operational time?

-----

## Operational Problem

During the scenario, the Pacific Theater Medical Hub serves as the primary blood-support hub for multiple forward spokes. A mass-casualty demand surge occurs after enemy action disrupts movement corridors and limits available lift. The hub remains physically operational, but its ability to sustain the network is degraded by competing demands, transportation delays, refrigeration limits, expiring inventory, and constrained resupply from outside the theater.

Several spoke nodes still appear green based on inventory alone; however, the model shows that blood-support risk is rising because products may expire before delivery, cold-chain capacity is insufficient, and route reliability is declining. A commander relying only on static inventory would miss the emerging risk. The prototype exposes the hidden operational problem: blood may exist somewhere in the network, but it may not be viable, transportable, or available in time.

-----

## Scenario Inject

A 14-day, medium-severity MASCAL demand surge is triggered at one forward Marine node. At the same time, primary sea and air routes experience delay and reduced reliability; refrigerated storage capacity at one spoke is degraded; blood collection and testing supplies are consumed faster than planned; a regional hub must choose whether to support the highest-casualty node or preserve broader network resilience; approved buy-on-market options are available for some support items, but not all blood products; and walking blood bank activation is possible at selected nodes, but donor screening kits and testing supplies are limited.

The model must identify which node becomes critical first, what causes the risk, and what mitigation action provides the greatest operational benefit.

-----

## Required Decision Output

The prototype should not only display risk. It should recommend a commander-facing course of action.

### Example Decision Output

**Node at Greatest Risk:** Forward Unit Blue

**Primary Failure Driver:** Blood collection kits and cold-chain packaging will be exhausted before additional products can arrive.

**Projected Mission Impact:** Blood-support failure within 5 days if no action is taken.

**Recommended COA:**

- Divert blood collection kits and cold-chain packaging from Regional Hub South.
- Shift movement to the secondary route despite lower reliability.
- Trigger approved buy-on-market sourcing for refrigeration support, validated shipping materials, dry ice, labels, and transfusion accessories.
- Preserve packed red blood cells for the highest-risk casualty node.
- Prepare walking blood bank activation, but do not execute until donor screening and testing supplies are confirmed.

**Operational Effect:** Restores 6–8 days of viable blood-support capacity and preserves commander flexibility during distributed operations.

-----

## Decision-Support Logic

The tool should calculate a **Blood Support Mission Risk Score** for each hub and spoke. Risk should be based on the following factors:

|Risk Factor |Why It Matters |
|----------------------------|------------------------------------------------|
|Inventory on hand |Shows available blood and support supplies |
|Expiration dates |Prevents false confidence in unusable products |
|Daily burn rate |Shows how quickly supplies are consumed |
|Casualty demand surge |Models MASCAL impact |
|Cold-chain status |Confirms whether blood remains viable |
|Route latency |Shows how long resupply will take |
|Route reliability |Shows probability that resupply will arrive |
|Lift availability |Determines whether supplies can move |
|Testing supplies |Enables safe use of collected blood |
|Walking blood bank readiness|Provides emergency whole-blood option |
|Approved vendor options |Identifies lawful and feasible market relief |
|Communications status |Determines whether updates and requests can flow|

### Risk Categories

|Status|Meaning |
|------|----------------------------------------------------------------|
|Green |Blood support is sufficient and transportable |
|Amber |Blood support is viable but fragile |
|Red |Blood support failure is projected within the decision window |
|Black |Blood support is unavailable, unusable, or cannot be transported|

## Data Inputs

- Blood and component inventory
- Expiration dates
- Blood collection and donor-screening capacity
- Testing supplies
- Cold-chain equipment status
- Refrigeration capacity
- Route status and route reliability
- Air, sea, and ground lift availability
- Casualty demand scenarios
- Node-level personnel and mission demand
- Approved buy-on-market vendors
- Host-nation or commercial sourcing options
- Walking blood bank readiness
- Communications status
- Generator, fuel, and power availability for refrigeration

-----

## Integration with Marine Corps Decision Systems

The prototype should be designed as a medical logistics data layer that could feed Maven or a similar operational common operating picture. This would allow blood-support risk to appear alongside force posture, route status, lift availability, casualty demand, and contested logistics constraints.

The purpose is not to create a separate medical dashboard that commanders must remember to check. The goal is to make blood-support risk visible inside the commander’s existing decision environment.

-----

## Human-in-the-Loop Requirement

The tool does not make clinical decisions or automatically allocate blood. It supports commander, medical, logistics, contracting, and blood-program decision-making by identifying risk and recommending options. Final decisions remain with authorized leaders and subject-matter experts.

-----

## Cybersecurity and Data Protection Requirement

Blood logistics data must be protected because it can reveal force posture, casualty trends, medical readiness, movement constraints, and operational vulnerability. The prototype should include role-based access, audit logs, data minimization, and clear separation between operational planning data and protected health information.

-----

|Operational relevance|Supports distributed Marine operations in contested logistics |

-----

## Closing Statement

Blood availability is not only a medical logistics issue. It is a combat-power preservation issue. When blood support fails, survivability drops, evacuation demand increases, and commanders lose freedom of maneuver.

Blood Support Mission Assurance converts blood logistics from a reactive resupply problem into a predictive mission-assurance capability for distributed Marine operations.
